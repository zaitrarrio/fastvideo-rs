Scope: what a small team can do to produce its own video model — from-scratch open recipes with published compute, continued-pretraining / architecture-extension examples that started from an open base, distillation and post-training as the product model, open datasets and their licences, multi-node trainers, October-2026 cloud prices, and the realistic quality outcome per budget tier. All web sources were accessed 2026-10-21. Dollar figures are quoted as the sources give them; every cost that is my own estimate is labelled "ESTIMATE" with the arithmetic shown.

## KQ1. What is the cheapest documented from-scratch recipe that produced a VBench-competitive video model, and what did it cost in GPU-hours and data?

### Takeaway
The cheapest fully documented from-scratch run that reaches Wan2.1-14B-level VBench numbers is NVIDIA's SANA-Video-2B: "12 days on 64 H100 GPUs" (= 18,432 H100-hours, my arithmetic) for VBench 83.71 (480p) / 84.05 (720p) versus Wan2.1-14B 83.69 / 83.73 — but it relies on linear attention, a very aggressive DCAE-V autoencoder, and an undisclosed pre-training dataset size. The cheapest full-attention, dollar-quoted recipe is Open-Sora 2.0 (11B): 4,160 H200 GPU-days ≈ $199.6k at the paper's assumed $2/H200-hour on 70M → 10M → 5M video clips; at October-2026 H200 rental prices the same run would cost roughly $320k–$630k (ESTIMATE below). Everything from the large labs that publishes compute (Seaweed-7B: 665,000 H100-hours; Wan 2.1: "O(1) trillions of tokens", GPU count undisclosed) is an order of magnitude beyond that.

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE — Open-Sora 2.0 at October-2026 prices: 4,160 H200 GPU-days × 24 = 99,840 H200-hours. At GMI "from $3.20" → $319k; at Nebius $5.40 → $539k; at Runpod H200 cluster $6.35 → $634k. The paper's $200k assumes $2/H200-hour, which no provider page I read offers on-demand in October 2026 (Nebius preemptible "from $0.79" is the only sub-$2 H200 rate, and it is spot).
- ESTIMATE — SANA-Video-2B: 12 × 24 × 64 = 18,432 H100-hours. At $2.60 → $47.9k; at $3.99 → $73.5k; at $5.85 (Lambda 64-GPU cluster) → $107.8k. This excludes the (undisclosed) data acquisition and captioning cost and assumes the 64-GPU run was the entire pre-training (the paper does not list separate stage budgets).
- ESTIMATE — Open-Sora 1.2: 35,000 H100-hours → $91k at $2.60, $140k at $3.99; but VBench 79.23 is ~4–5 points below 2025 models, so this is "working model", not "competitive".
- ESTIMATE — Seaweed-7B: 665,000 H100-hours → $1.73M at $2.60, $2.65M at $3.99, $3.89M at $5.85. This is the floor for a "frontier-adjacent" 7B dense model from a lab with a 100M-clip pipeline; it excludes the data pipeline, which processes 500k hours/day.
- The two sub-$250k recipes (SANA-Video, Open-Sora 2.0) both lean on a high-compression autoencoder (DCAE-V 32× spatial; Video DC-AE 4×32×32) to cut token counts (Open-Sora 2.0: 76K → 19K tokens for a 5 s 768px clip), and both do the bulk of training at ≤256p. That is the transferable lesson: compute scales with tokens, and the autoencoder choice is the lever.
- Wan 2.1/2.2 and HunyuanVideo publish no GPU-hour figures; Wan's "O(1) trillion tokens" cannot be turned into a cost without an assumed throughput, so a "Wan-recipe" from scratch at Wan scale has no documented price (see KQ5).

- SANA-Video's pre-training dataset size and whether the 64-H100/12-day figure covers every stage are not stated.

## KQ2. What examples exist of successfully extending an open base into a new capability (audio, long video, streaming, higher resolution), and what compute did they use?

### Takeaway
Every documented extension of an open base in 2025–26 starts from Wan (2.1 1.3B/14B or 2.2 5B/14B) and the only ones that publish compute are the autoregressive/streaming conversions: LongLive ("32 H100 GPU-days" to take Wan2.1-1.3B to minute-long interactive video), Self-Forcing ("approximately 1.5 hours on 64 H100 GPUs" per DMD experiment), and Krea Realtime ("3k steps using 128 H100 GPUs" of causal ODE pre-training on Wan2.1-14B). Audio extensions (Ovi: Wan2.2-5B + a 5B audio twin trained from scratch on "hundreds of thousands of hours" of speech; Wan-S2V; HuMo) and world-model extensions (LingBot-World, 2×Wan2.2-I2V-14B experts) give step counts and batch sizes but no GPU counts.

### Cited Findings

Audio / talking-video extensions
- Ovi: "The video branch is initialized from Wan2.2 5B"; audio tower "architecture identical to that of a strong pretrained video model", "Trained from scratch on hundreds of thousands of hours of raw audio" ("primarily speech data up to 12 seconds"); audio pretraining 50k steps, batch 2880, lr 1e-4, bf16 + DeepSpeed; fusion 40k steps, batch 768, lr 5e-5; "a 11B symmetric twin backbone", "5.7B of 11B parameters trainable"; fusion via "blockwise cross-modal fusion of twin-DiT modules", scaled-RoPE for timing + bidirectional cross-attention; no GPU count or duration — [Ovi, arXiv 2510.01284 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01284); submitted 30 Sep 2025 — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01284). Attention / architecture surgery on an existing DiT
- VDN (Video DeltaNet) on MiniMax H3 (Sep 2026, arXiv 2609.20744): hybrid "softmax attention for nearby frames with a linear 'Video Delta Attention' branch for long-range context"; "with eight-step distillation, VDN-H3 completes DiT denoising for a 14.3-second, 768p video in 6.70 seconds on eight NVIDIA B200 GPUs", "14.5× reduction relative to the 50-step dense H3 baseline" — [alphaxiv 2609.20744](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20744.md); README: "starts from its released transformer weights"; adds "a separate linear attention branch and two small LoRA adapters" (4.3 GB / 5.1 GB); training steps per stage 200 (A1), 500 (A2), 2,000 (B), 250 (DMD); no GPU count/hours; training code in `src/training/`; code Apache-2.0, weights "MiniMax H3 Community License Agreement"; 8×B200 SGLang: 14.4 s clip "in about 9.0 seconds end-to-end" — [OpenVDN README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenVDN/vdn-minimax-h3/main/README.md). A secondary report claims the MiniMax H3 Community License excludes EU/UK/South Korea/US — [aiweekly.co](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/openvdns-vdn-minimax-h3-generates-144s-of-768p-in-1123s) (not verified in the licence text).
- VAE swap inside one project: Open-Sora 2.0 "Starts with the HunyuanVideo VAE, then moves to Video DC-AE" (4×32×32) at the 768px stage — [arXiv 2503.09642](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.09642). SkyReels-V2 keeps Wan's VAE and text encoder while re-training the DiT — [arXiv 2504.13074v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13074v3). Ovi reuses MMAudio's audio VAE — [github.com/character-ai/Ovi](https://github.com/character-ai/Ovi).

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE — LongLive: 32 H100 GPU-days = 768 H100-hours → $2.0k at $2.60, $3.1k at $3.99, $4.5k at $5.85. ESTIMATE — Self-Forcing: 1.5 h × 64 = 96 H100-hours (paper) or <2 h × 64 = <128 H100-hours (repo) → $250–$510. These are the only extension examples priced below $10k from published numbers.
- ESTIMATE — Krea Realtime causal pretraining: 3k steps on 128 H100 with no duration stated; if a step took 10–30 s (my assumption, not sourced), that is 128 × 3,000 × (10–30 s) / 3600 ≈ 1,070–3,200 H100-hours ≈ $2.8k–$12.8k at $2.60–$3.99. Treat as order-of-magnitude only.
- Audio extension cost is dominated by the audio tower, not the fusion: Ovi's audio pre-training (50k steps × batch 2880 = 144M samples) is ~4.7× more sample-steps than its fusion stage (40k × 768 = 30.7M). Without a GPU count no dollar figure can be derived; the fact that Ovi has not released training scripts (TODO unchecked) means the recipe would have to be re-implemented.
- Pattern: the cheap, reproducible extensions (Self-Forcing, LongLive, Krea, rCM) are "data-free" or prompt-only — they need VidProM-style prompts and the teacher, not a video corpus. Extensions that add a modality (Ovi, Wan-S2V, HuMo) need O(50k)–O(1M) paired clips plus hundreds of thousands of hours of audio.
- I found no example of a published project swapping the text encoder of an existing open video DiT and reporting the cost; VAE swaps are done inside full re-trainings (Open-Sora 2.0) rather than as a retrofit.

### Gaps
- Ovi, Wan-S2V, HuMo, SkyReels-V2 (base pretrain), LingBot-World, VDN: no GPU count or GPU-hours published.
- Krea Realtime: GPU-hours of the Self-Forcing DMD stage not stated (the blog mentions 64 H100 only in an OOM anecdote).
- MiniMax H3's own parameter count and the licence's geographic terms were not in the README; the EU/UK/KR/US exclusion claim is secondary and unverified.
- Could not retrieve primeintellect pricing page or Prime Intellect's H100 page (404s), see KQ5.

## KQ3. Which open datasets and prompt sets can be used commercially, and which have non-commercial licences?

### Takeaway
Essentially every large open video-text dataset derived from YouTube inherits the HD-VILA-100M "R-UDA" terms ("use the Data solely for Computational Use for non-commercial research"; "you may not use the Data or any Results in any commercial offering", where "Artificial intelligence models trained on Data" are Results) — this covers Panda-70M, Koala-36M and the Panda-derived part of OpenVid-1M. InternVid is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, VidProM (prompts and Pika videos) is CC BY-NC 4.0, Vript is "academic research purposes only", and MiraData is tagged GPL-3.0. OpenVid-1M carries a cc-by-4.0 tag but its own card says "intended for research and non-commercial purposes". I found no large open video dataset whose card affirmatively permits commercial model training; the published commercial-grade models (Seaweed, Wan, Kandinsky, LTX) all describe in-house or licensed corpora.

### Cited Findings
- HD-VILA-100M licence (Microsoft R-UDA): "You agree that you will use the Data solely for Computational Use for non-commercial research"; "you may not use the Data or any Results in any commercial offering"; "You may not receive money or other consideration in exchange for use or redistribution of Data"; redistribution allowed only if you "bind each recipient … to the terms of the R-UDA"; Section 5.5: "Artificial intelligence models trained on Data" are treated as Results — [HD-VILA-100M LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/XPretrain/main/hd-vila-100m/LICENSE)
- Panda-70M: 70,723,513 clips from 3,779,763 source videos (plus 10M and 2M subsets); "The video samples are collected from a publicly available dataset"; "Users must follow [the related license] to use these video samples", linking the HD-VILA-100M LICENSE above; no commercial-use statement — [github.com/snap-research/Panda-70M](https://github.com/snap-research/Panda-70M)
- Koala-36M: "over 10M" videos stated on the page (36M in the name); captions "over 200 words"; "Users must follow the related license", again linking HD-VILA-100M — [github.com/KwaiVGI/Koala-36M](https://github.com/KwaiVGI/Koala-36M) (HF dataset page returned HTTP 401)
- OpenVid-1M: card metadata "cc-by-4.0", but text: "Our OpenVid-1M dataset is released under the CC-BY-4.0 license and is intended for research and non-commercial purposes"; videos "collected from publicly available datasets" — Panda, ChronoMagic, Open-Sora-plan, CelebvHQ (licence "Unknow"); "All videos … have resolutions of at least 512×512"; OpenVidHD = "433K 1080p videos" — [HF nkp37/OpenVid-1M](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nkp37/OpenVid-1M)

- Prompt sets used for data-free distillation/RL: Self-Forcing and Krea use filtered/extended VidProM; DanceGRPO "curated from VidProM", "more than 10,000 prompts" — [Self Forcing](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08009v2), [Krea blog](https://www.krea.ai/blog/krea-realtime-14b), [DanceGRPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07818)
- Output-use restriction on a base model: HunyuanVideo licence "must not use the Tencent Hunyuan Works or any Output or results" to improve other AI models (except Hunyuan derivatives); Territory excludes EU, UK, South Korea; >100M MAU requires a licence from Tencent — [HunyuanVideo LICENSE.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent/HunyuanVideo/main/LICENSE.txt). Wan 2.1 / 2.2 weights: Apache 2.0 — [Wan2.1-T2V-14B](https://huggingface.co/Wan-AI/Wan2.1-T2V-14B), [Wan2.2-TI2V-5B](https://huggingface.co/Wan-AI/Wan2.2-TI2V-5B)

### Inferences
- For a commercial model, the open YouTube-derived corpora (Panda-70M, Koala-36M, OpenVid-1M, InternVid, HD-VILA, Vript) are usable at most for research prototypes/ablations; the R-UDA's "Results" clause explicitly reaches trained models. A team must budget for licensed stock footage or its own crawl plus captioning (Seaweed's pipeline captions 500k hours/day; SkyReels trained its captioner on 64 A800) — none of the recipes above publish that cost.
- VidProM prompts are CC BY-NC, so the data-free distillation recipes (Self-Forcing, Krea, DanceGRPO) as published are non-commercial on the prompt side; a commercial reproduction needs its own prompt set (Krea's blog mentions generating synthetic prompts, which is the obvious workaround).
- Distilling from Wan (Apache 2.0) is unencumbered on the teacher side; distilling from HunyuanVideo is contractually blocked for a non-Hunyuan student by the "Output" clause.
- MiraData's GPL-3.0 tag is unusual for data; whether GPL's copyleft meaningfully attaches to a trained model is legally unsettled — flag for counsel rather than assume either way.

### Gaps
- The Open-Sora data pipeline's own licence and the exact source list for Open-Sora 2.0's 70M clips were not found (the paper gives counts only).
- Koala-36M's HF card (401) and MiraData's full card text (truncated) were not readable; MiraData's commercial statement, if any, is unknown.
- CelebvHQ licence listed as "Unknow" on the OpenVid card.
- I did not find a licence text for LTX-2 (three LICENSE files in the repo, not quoted) or for Ovi's weights.

## KQ4. Which trainers are production-ready for multi-node video DiT training today?

### Takeaway
Only a handful of open trainers have documented multi-node video-DiT runs: Open-Sora (ColossalAI; the 2.0 paper trained 11B on 192–224 H200 with CP=4), NVIDIA NeMo's diffusion framework (Megatron-based; DiT/MovieGen 30B+, benchmarked on 32 H100 DGX nodes with CP), and the Self-Forcing/LongLive codebase (launches with `--nnodes=8 --nproc_per_node=8`). FastVideo advertises "FSDP2, sequence parallelism, and selective activation checkpointing" plus DMD2/Self-Forcing/Attn-QAT for Wan 2.1/2.2 and HunyuanVideo 1.5, but its finetune docs show only `--nnodes 1`; DiffSynth-Studio (Apache-2.0) covers the widest model list (Wan 2.2, LTX-2, MiniMax-H3, LingBot-Video) with full/LoRA training and OPD distillation but does not document DeepSpeed/FSDP/multi-node in the README. No TorchTitan video trainer and no Lingbot trainer were found; MAGI-1 and Lingbot ship inference code only.

### Cited Findings
- FastVideo (Hao AI Lab): "Full finetuning and LoRA finetuning for state-of-the-art open video DiTs"; "Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD2) stepwise distillation"; "Causal distillation through Self-Forcing"; "Scalable training with FSDP2, sequence parallelism, and selective activation checkpointing"; "Attn-QAT training for quantization-aware post-training"; "Sparse distillation to achieve >50x denoising speedup" — [haoailab.com/FastVideo](https://haoailab.com/FastVideo/). Training overview: methods "Full finetune, LoRA finetune, VSA finetune, PDD/DMPDD, and Attn-QAT"; precomputed text embeddings + latents stored as Parquet; models named: Wan2.1, Wan2.2 A14B I2V, HunyuanVideo 1.5 T2V, MiniMax-H3 (PDD/DMPDD) — [training/overview](https://haoailab.com/FastVideo/training/overview/). Finetune page: examples Wan2.1 T2V 1.3B, Wan2.1 I2V 14B, Wan2.1-Fun 1.3B InP, Wan2.1 VSA, Wan2.1 T2V 1.3B Attn-QAT; `--sp_size` = "Sequence parallel size (increase to reduce memory per GPU)"; example `torchrun --nnodes 1 --nproc_per_node 4`; "LoRA … Can run on fewer GPUs (even single GPU)"; no multi-node/Slurm instructions or throughput on the page — [training/finetune](https://haoailab.com/FastVideo/training/finetune/)

- TorchTitan: search for a torchtitan video-diffusion/DiT trainer returned no results — [search result summary](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/25.09/vision/diffusionmodels/dit.html) (only NeMo, DualParal and xDiT came back)

### Inferences
- "Production-ready multi-node" with published evidence today means: Open-Sora (ColossalAI SP, 11B on 224 GPUs), NeMo diffusion (Megatron; 28B at 64k context on 256 H100), and the Self-Forcing/LongLive codebase for AR distillation (64 H100). FastVideo's FSDP2 + SP claims are credible but its public docs stop at one node; DiffSynth-Studio is the broadest "it has a script" trainer but documents no scale-out.
- For a Wan-weights-based product model the practical stack is: DiffSynth-Studio or FastVideo for SFT/LoRA; FastVideo or the Self-Forcing repo for DMD/Self-Forcing distillation; NVlabs/rcm for consistency distillation of Wan 14B (CP=10 documented); DanceGRPO-style RL needs its own fork (64 H800 reported).
- A from-scratch Wan-recipe run at ≥11B needs CP=4+ at 768px (Open-Sora 2.0 Table 2) — only Open-Sora, NeMo and the MAGI/Wan-style 2D-CP stacks document that.

### Gaps
- FastVideo multi-node (Slurm, nnodes>1) documentation and throughput numbers were not found on the pages read; a "Training Infrastructure" page is referenced but was not fetched.
- DiffSynth-Studio's multi-GPU backend (DeepSpeed vs FSDP) and multi-node status not visible in the README excerpt.
- No TorchTitan video trainer and no Lingbot trainer found; MAGI-1 training code status unstated.
- No MFU/throughput figures for any open trainer on a Wan-size model (only the papers' internal MFU: Seaweed 38%, Waver 32–40%).

## KQ6 (supplementary). Distillation and post-training as the product model — what do DMD2, Self-Forcing, rCM and RL cost?

### Takeaway
Few-step distillation of an open Wan checkpoint is the cheapest documented path to a product-grade model: Self-Forcing converges in ~96–128 H100-hours and matches/exceeds the teacher on VBench (84.31 vs 84.26); rCM distils Wan2.1-14B to 4 steps in 10k iterations (batch 64, CP=10) and reports VBench 84.92 vs the 83.58 teacher; DMD2's image-scale reference is 64 A100 × ~60 h for SDXL. RL (DanceGRPO) used 64 H800 for HunyuanVideo and 32 H800 for SkyReels-I2V with no hours given; VideoReward's preference set is 182k triplets from 108k videos / 16k prompts.

### Cited Findings
- DMD2 (image): SDXL "train the model on 64 A100 GPUs for 20K iterations for the 4-step generator and 25K iterations for the 1-step generator, taking approximately 60 hours"; ImageNet "7 A100 GPUs for 200K iterations, which takes approximately 2 days"; GAN discriminator uses "500k images from LAION-Aesthetic", "does not require paired data" — [DMD2, arXiv 2405.14867](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14867)
- Self-Forcing: "approximately 1.5 hours on 64 H100 GPUs"; VBench 84.31 (chunk-wise) / 84.26 (frame-wise) vs Wan2.1-1.3B 84.26; prompts from VidProM; GAN variant used 70k videos generated by the 14B model — [arXiv 2506.08009v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08009v2); repo: 600 iterations, "under 2 hours using 64 H100 GPUs"; "data-free" — [github.com/guandeh17/Self-Forcing](https://github.com/guandeh17/Self-Forcing)
- rCM (NVIDIA, ICLR 2026): Wan2.1 1.3B — 10k iterations, batch 256; Wan2.1 14B — 10k iterations, batch 64, "context parallel size 10"; Cosmos-Predict2 0.6B/2B/14B 80k/30k/25k iterations; "supplemented with synthetic data generated by Wan2.1 T2V 14B"; VBench 480p: Wan2.1-1.3B teacher 83.02 (0.72 FPS) → rCM 4-step 84.43 (14.6 FPS); Wan2.1-14B teacher 83.58 (0.18 FPS) → rCM 4-step 84.92 (4.5 FPS); "15×∼50×" speed-up; FlashAttention-2 JVP kernel enables sCM on >10B — [rCM, arXiv 2510.08431v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08431v2); code github.com/NVlabs/rcm — [NVIDIA page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/cosmos-lab/rcm/)
- DanceGRPO: "64 H800 GPUs for text-to-video generation systems" (HunyuanVideo); "32 H800 GPUs for image-to-video" (SkyReels-I2V); "more than 10,000 prompts" (VidProM-curated); reward VideoAlign (VQ + MQ); HunyuanVideo VQ 4.51 → 7.03 (+56%), MQ 1.37 → 3.85 (+181%), TA 1.75 → 1.59; SkyReels-I2V MQ +118%; hours not reported — [DanceGRPO, arXiv 2505.07818](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07818). Third-party: FLUX run "within 12 hours on 16 H800 GPUs" — [sourcepulse](https://www.sourcepulse.org/projects/13811141). MixGRPO HunyuanVideo-1.5 experiment "on 64 Nvidia H800 GPUs" — [arXiv 2507.21802](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.21802)
- VideoReward / Flow-DPO (Kuaishou, NeurIPS 2025): "12 T2V models to generate 108k videos from 16k unique prompts, resulting in 182k annotated triplets"; reward backbone Qwen2-VL-2B; alignment run on "an internal, research-purpose video generation model"; no GPU figures — [arXiv 2501.13918v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13918v2)
- SkyReels-V2 RL: Qwen2.5-VL-7B reward on ~30k pairs; 8 samples per prompt; "a total of 3 stages of DPO training" — [arXiv 2504.13074v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13074v3)
- Seaweed-7B distillation targets ~24 NFE then 8 NFE with adversarial training; 12 NFE in 29.6 s on one H100 vs Wan2.1-14B 100 NFE 1837.9 s; SFT on 256 GPUs; no hours — [arXiv 2504.08685](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08685)
- Kandinsky 5.0 distillation: CFG distillation + trajectory-segmented consistency + adversarial post-training, NFE 100 → 16 — [arXiv 2511.14993](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.14993)
- MAGI-1 uses shortcut-model distillation (64/32/16/8 steps) — [arXiv 2505.13211](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13211)
- Trainer support: FastVideo DMD2 + Self-Forcing + sparse distillation; DiffSynth-Studio OPD — see KQ4.

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE — Self-Forcing product model: 96–128 H100-h ≈ $250–$510; even 20 full re-runs for hyper-parameter search stay under $10k.
- ESTIMATE — rCM on Wan 14B: 10k iterations with CP=10 implies ≥10 GPUs per sample; at an assumed 10–20 s/iteration on 64 H100 (unsourced), 64 × 10,000 × (10–20)/3600 ≈ 1,800–3,600 H100-h ≈ $4.6k–$14k.
- ESTIMATE — DanceGRPO on a 13B model: 64 H800 × (assumed 24–72 h, unsourced; the FLUX run was 16 GPUs × 12 h) ≈ 1,500–4,600 GPU-h ≈ $4k–$18k at H100-equivalent prices.
- The distilled student routinely scores above the teacher on VBench (Self-Forcing, rCM), so a distilled Wan is a credible "own product model" at a four-figure GPU bill; the licence exposure is the teacher's (Apache-2.0 for Wan; prohibited for Hunyuan under its Output clause) and the prompt set's (VidProM CC BY-NC).

### Gaps
- No GPU-hours for DanceGRPO, rCM, VideoReward/Flow-DPO, Seaweed SFT/DPO/distillation, Kandinsky distillation/RL.
- Flow-GRPO (the named method) did not surface in results; only Flow-DPO/Flow-RWR (VideoReward paper) and DanceGRPO/MixGRPO.

## KQ7 (supplementary). Realistic quality outcome per budget tier ($10k / $100k / $1M), based on what published projects achieved

### Takeaway
$10k buys a distilled/streaming/long-video derivative of Wan (Self-Forcing, LongLive, rCM) that matches or beats the base on VBench — not a new base model. $100k buys a ~2B from-scratch model that is VBench-competitive only with SANA-Video's efficiency tricks (18.4k H100-h ≈ $48k–$108k) or a 1B Open-Sora-1.2-class model (35k H100-h, VBench 79). $1M buys an Open-Sora-2.0-class 11B dense model (≈$320k–$630k at 2026 prices, VBench ~83.6 by secondary report, "comparable to HunyuanVideo") with headroom for data and reruns, but not a Seaweed/Wan/Kandinsky-Pro-class model, which need ≥665k H100-h and 100M+-clip pipelines.

### Cited Findings
- $10k-tier precedents: Self-Forcing 64 H100 × 1.5 h, VBench 84.31 vs teacher 84.26 — [arXiv 2506.08009v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08009v2); LongLive 32 H100 GPU-days → 240 s videos at 20.7 FPS — [arXiv 2509.22622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22622); rCM Wan-1.3B 4-step VBench 84.43 vs 83.02 — [arXiv 2510.08431v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08431v2); DMD2 SDXL 64 A100 × 60 h — [arXiv 2405.14867](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14867)
- $100k-tier precedents: SANA-Video-2B 64 H100 × 12 d, VBench 83.71/84.05 vs Wan2.1-14B 83.69/83.73 — [arXiv 2509.24695v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24695v2); Open-Sora 1.2 35k H100-h, VBench 79.23 — [report_03](https://github.com/hpcaitech/Open-Sora/blob/main/docs/report_03.md)
- $1M-tier precedents: Open-Sora 2.0 $199.6k at $2/H200-h, 4,160 GPU-days, 70M clips, "comparable to … HunyuanVideo and … Runway Gen-3 Alpha", Sora gap 0.69% — [arXiv 2503.09642](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.09642), [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09642); secondary total 83.6 — [the-decoder](https://the-decoder.com/open-sora-2-0-achieves-competitive-ai-video-quality-at-one-tenth-the-training-cost-of-commercial-models/)
- Above $1M: Seaweed-7B 665,000 H100-h — [arXiv 2504.08685](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08685); Kandinsky 5.0 Pro 19B on >250M video scenes, 10 PB — [arXiv 2511.14993](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.14993); Wan 2.1 "O(1) trillions of tokens" — [arXiv 2503.20314](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20314); "MovieGen uses 6,000+ NVIDIA H100 GPUs" — [Seaweed](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08685)

### Inferences
- Tier mapping (ESTIMATE, GPU time at $2.60–$3.99/H100-h):
  - $10k: 2,500–3,800 H100-h → multiple Self-Forcing/rCM/LongLive runs on Wan 1.3B/5B, one rCM pass on Wan 14B, or a modest LoRA/SFT; quality = Wan base ± VBench 1 point, new capability (streaming, 4-step, minute-long).
  - $100k: 25k–38k H100-h → one SANA-Video-scale 2B pretrain (with little margin for reruns) or one Open-Sora-1.2-scale 1B pretrain with reruns; quality ≈ Wan2.1-1.3B/14B VBench only if the efficient architecture is reproduced, else 2024-era.
  - $1M: 250k–385k H100-h → one Open-Sora-2.0-scale 11B run (~100k H200-h ≈ 120k H100-h assumed) plus data pipeline and 1–2 reruns; quality ≈ HunyuanVideo/early-2025 open SOTA, below Wan 2.2 / Kandinsky Pro / Seaweed.
- Data, not GPU-hours, is the binding constraint at $100k+: every ≥11B precedent used 70M–250M clips; the licensed-for-commerce fraction of open data is ~zero (KQ3).

### Gaps
- No published project sits squarely at the $10k from-scratch tier; the smallest documented from-scratch runs are SANA-Video (18k H100-h) and Open-Sora 1.2 (35k H100-h).
- No VBench numbers for Kandinsky 5.0, Waver, MAGI-1, Wan 2.2, LTX-2 were found in the sources read, so the ">$1M" tier is characterised by data/compute scale rather than by a common benchmark.
