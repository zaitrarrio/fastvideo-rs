# Wan 3.0 and the open-weight video models we could iterate on

Owner question (2026-10-21): research Wan 3.0 and its methodology and techniques, to see whether what we know about
Wan can be leveraged to pursue our own implementation, or whether there are other high-quality open-weight models we
can iterate on. This is desk research only: nothing was trained, built or run on a GPU, and no weights were
downloaded. Every claim below carries its source URL; the per-topic research notes with the full citation trail are
in [`wan3-and-open-video-models/`](wan3-and-open-video-models/) (Wan 3.0 status, the Wan 2.x open recipe, the Wan
derivative ecosystem, the open-weight landscape, own-model training paths and costs). Prices and leaderboard
positions are as read on 2026-10-21. Companion document: [h3-training-cost.md](h3-training-cost.md).

---

## Summary: Wan 3.0 stays closed; distil Wan 2.2 instead

Alibaba has published nothing technical about Wan 3.0: no paper, model card, parameter count, architecture or training description exists, the model is API-only ("Currently in preview" in the Model Studio reference as of its 2026-10-15 revision), and the official Hugging Face and GitHub organisations still end at Wan 2.2-generation weights, so there is no Wan 3.0 recipe to reproduce and no promise of one ([Model Studio API reference](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/wan3-video-generation-api-reference); [huggingface.co/Wan-AI](https://huggingface.co/Wan-AI)). What is public is the Wan 2.1 technical report and the Wan 2.2 configs, which fully specify the architecture and objective but omit dataset sizes, GPU-hours, batch sizes and any training code; everything that trains a Wan model today is third-party (DiffSynth, musubi, finetrainers, FastVideo). That gap decides the strategy: a from-scratch "Wan-recipe" model costs **$48k-108k for a 2B model that only reaches Wan 2.1-1.3B quality, $320k-630k for an 11B model at 2025 quality, and multi-million dollars at Wan-14B scale**, and every open video corpus large enough to feed it is licensed non-commercially. By contrast the Wan derivative ecosystem shows that a data-free few-step or streaming student of Wan 2.1/2.2 is a **$250-4,000 job on one 8-GPU pod** that matches the teacher on VBench. The catch is the ceiling: Wan 2.2 is two closed generations behind Wan 3.0 (AA Elo 1173) and the only open model at the frontier is MiniMax H3 (1158), whose licence excludes the US/EU/UK/Korea; the clean-licence open tier (MAGI-2 1044, LTX-2.5 ~950-984, Kandinsky 6.0 unranked) sits 110-200 Elo lower ([AA T2V leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/text-to-video)). Our recommendation, ranked below, is to pursue two cheap distillation pilots (a Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B data-free DMD/VSA student, ~$250-400, and a Wan 2.1-1.3B Self-Forcing/LongLive streaming model on a commercially clean prompt set, ~$250-510), keep the already-costed H3 LoRA-DMD pilot (~$300) as the quality play if the owner's MiniMax licence covers derivatives, and not pursue any from-scratch pretraining, any Wan 3.0 "reproduction", or a 14B causal conversion until a public trainer exists.

## Wan 3.0 is a hosted product with no published method

Wan 3.0 ("Wan3.0-Video", model IDs `wan3.0-video` and `wan3.0-video-prime`) entered public beta in China on **2026-08-06** and was announced internationally on **2026-08-13** as "now available on Alibaba Cloud Model Studio"; the English API reference revised 2026-10-15 still labels it "Currently in preview" ([QbitAI, 2026-08-07](https://www.qbitai.com/2026/08/467877.html); [Alibaba Cloud blog, 2026-08-13](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/wan3-0-30-second-ai-video-generation-from-any-input_603452); [Model Studio API reference](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/wan3-video-generation-api-reference)). Everything Alibaba says about it is product-level: up to **30 seconds in a single pass at 30 fps and 1080p**, audio on by default (stereo, priced identically with or without), a `-1` "smart duration" mode, video extension, and an "omni-reference" input set of up to 10 images, 5 videos (≤15 s total), 5 audio clips, one document (doc/xls/ppt/pdf/md, ≤100 MB or 50 pages) and one web link, with a 20,000-character prompt limit ([help.aliyun.com API reference](https://help.aliyun.com/zh/model-studio/wan3-video-generation-api-reference)). International list price is **$0.05 / $0.10 / $0.20 per output second at 480P / 720P / 1080P** ($6.00 for 30 s at 1080P), roughly the CNY 0.3/0.6/1.2 domestic price plus a markup, and 2× the Wan 2.6/2.7 1080P rate ([Alibaba Cloud blog](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/wan3-0-30-second-ai-video-generation-from-any-input_603452); [developer.aliyun.com/article/1754056](https://developer.aliyun.com/article/1754056)). The one quasi-technical remark in Alibaba-provided content attributes the gains to "strong prompt engineering and instruction-following that convert prompts into scheduling inputs", which reads as an LLM planning front end rather than an architecture statement ([QbitAI](https://www.qbitai.com/2026/08/467877.html)). Alibaba's own Chinese developer article states plainly that Wan 3.0 is not open source and has no weights, Hugging Face release or ComfyUI plugin ([developer.aliyun.com/article/1754056](https://developer.aliyun.com/article/1754056)).

The closure is not specific to 3.0. Wan 2.5-Preview (Sep 2025, first native joint audio), Wan 2.6 (Dec 2025, reference-to-video, multi-shot), Wan 2.7 (Apr 2026, four-model suite, "thinking mode" documented only for the image API) and Wan 3.0 have all shipped as Model Studio endpoints with no report, parameter count or weights, and community GitHub issues asking about open weights have sat unanswered since September 2025 ([Wan2.6 blog](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/alibaba-unveils-wan2-6-series-enabling-everyone-to-star-in-videos_602742); [Wan2.7 blog](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/alibaba-unveils-wan2-7-video-to-elevate-creators-from-executors-to-directors_603009); [howaiworks.ai survey](https://howaiworks.ai/blog/alibaba-wan-open-weights-stopped-at-2-2)). SEO sites asserting that Wan 3.0 shipped Apache-2.0 1.3B/14B checkpoints in April 2026, or that it has "native 4K", "Identity Lock" or a specific parameter count, cite no Alibaba source and are contradicted by the official repos, where the newest entries are auxiliary models that carry 2.2-era names: **Wan2.2-Animate-2-14B** (2026-08-07, Apache-2.0, base plus a 10-step CFG-free distilled checkpoint; its streaming "Lite" variant was trained with Self-Forcing distillation but has no public weights) and **Wan-Dancer-14B** (July 2026, Apache-2.0, built on Wan 2.1) ([Wan-Animate-2 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Wan-Video/Wan-Animate-2/main/README.md); [arXiv 2608.06009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06009); [Wan-Dancer README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Wan-Video/Wan-Dancer/main/README.md); [Digital Applied reality check](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/wan-3-0-reality-check-rumor-vs-shipped)). Those two releases are the only evidence of what Tongyi Lab does internally: it uses few-step DMD-style and Self-Forcing distillation on 14B DiTs, which makes it plausible that `wan3.0-video-prime` is a step-distilled serving variant, but Alibaba has not said so, and nothing links Wan 3.0 to the Wan 2.2 two-expert MoE design.

On quality, Wan 3.0 is **#1 on the Artificial Analysis text-to-video arena (Elo 1173)** and #5 on image-to-video (1127); Wan 2.7 sits at #14/1040 and #13/1034, and no open Wan 2.2 model appears in the top 29 ([AA T2V](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/text-to-video); [AA I2V](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/image-to-video)). The ~130-Elo jump from 2.7 to 3.0 is the largest single-generation step in the Wan line on that board; some of it is likely the 30 s/30 fps/1080p/audio defaults winning pairwise votes rather than per-frame quality, since the arena ranks "video-with-audio" outputs. Alibaba itself concedes that "audio texture and on-screen text rendering accuracy are improving but not yet where we want them" ([Alibaba Cloud blog](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/wan3-0-30-second-ai-video-generation-from-any-input_603452)). The practical conclusion for us is that there is no Wan 3.0 methodology to leverage; what we can leverage is the open Wan 2.1/2.2 line and the techniques the community has built on it.

## The open Wan recipe specifies the model, not the run

The Wan 2.1 technical report plus the official config files pin down the architecture completely: a pre-norm DiT with full 3D attention, 3D RoPE (22/21/21 complex pairs for t/h/w at head dim 128), umT5-XXL cross-attention on 512 tokens, a shared adaLN MLP predicting six modulation parameters with per-block learned biases ("about a 25% parameter reduction"), patch (1,2,2) over a 4×8×8 causal VAE with 16 latent channels and only 127M parameters; the 1.3B model is dim 1536 / 30 layers / 12 heads, the 14B is dim 5120 / 40 layers / 40 heads ([arXiv 2503.20314](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20314); [Wan2.1 README](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.1); [wan_t2v_1_3B.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.1/main/wan/configs/wan_t2v_1_3B.py)). Wan 2.2 adds two things, both without a report of their own: the **A14B "MoE"**, which is not token-routed but two full 14B DiTs selected by timestep (high-noise expert for t ≥ 875/1000 on T2V, ≥ 900 on I2V; "27B total, 14B active"), and **TI2V-5B**, a dense 30-layer/3072-dim model on a new 4×16×16 VAE that attends over roughly 27k tokens for a 121-frame 720p clip against ~76k for the 14B's 81-frame 720p clip ([Wan2.2 README](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2); [wan_t2v_A14B.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2/main/wan/configs/wan_t2v_A14B.py); [wan_ti2v_5B.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2/main/wan/configs/wan_ti2v_5B.py)). All weights are Apache-2.0 ([HF Wan2.2-TI2V-5B](https://huggingface.co/Wan-AI/Wan2.2-TI2V-5B)).

The objective and curriculum are also public: flow matching with velocity MSE and logit-normal timesteps, bf16 AdamW at lr 1e-4 with weight decay 1e-3, a curriculum of 256px text-to-image, then 192px/16 fps/5 s video, then 480p, then 720p, then joint 480p/720p post-training on a "top 20%" curated set; the data pipeline is described qualitatively (a four-step cleaning that removes ~50% of raw data, 100-cluster sampling, six-tier motion classification, an in-house Qwen-based captioner, synthetic-image contamination "<10%" already degrading the model) ([arXiv 2503.20314](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20314)). What is not public is everything that sets the price: dataset sizes beyond "billions of images and videos" and "O(1) trillions of tokens", per-stage batch sizes and step counts, the LR schedule, any training-time timestep shift, cluster size, GPU type, GPU-hours or MFU, and any RL/DPO stage (none is described). Wan 2.2 adds only "+65.6% images / +83.2% videos" and aesthetic labels ([Wan2.2 README](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2)). The published ablations that transfer are modest: prefer depth over per-layer adaLN parameters, umT5 beats Qwen-VL/Qwen2.5/GLM encoders by ~0.1-0.8 FID, the two-expert timestep split lowers validation loss at constant per-step compute, and 14B beats 1.3B by only 0.035 on Wan-Bench and 2.26 VBench points, which is the report's own argument that a well-trained 1-2B dense DiT is competitive ([arXiv 2503.20314](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20314); [HF Wan2.2-T2V-A14B](https://huggingface.co/Wan-AI/Wan2.2-T2V-A14B)).

No official Wan repository contains training code; the READMEs document `generate.py` with FSDP + Ulysses inference only ([Wan2.1 README](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.1)). Training lives in DiffSynth-Studio (full and LoRA for every 2.1/2.2 variant, DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 for 14B full fine-tune, expert-split LoRA scripts), musubi-tuner (LoRA only, 24 GB consumer workflows), finetrainers (Wan 2.1 full/LoRA) and FastVideo (full/LoRA/VSA/DMD2 with HSDP + sequence parallel), and none of them documents from-scratch pre-training at Wan scale ([DiffSynth Wan docs](https://diffsynth-studio-doc.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Model_Details/Wan.html); [musubi-tuner docs/wan.md](https://github.com/kohya-ss/musubi-tuner/blob/main/docs/wan.md); [FastVideo train_infra](https://haoailab.com/FastVideo/training/train_infra/)). A "Wan recipe" in the reproducible sense therefore means: the architecture, the objective and the curriculum shape, built on third-party trainers whose public multi-node evidence stops at the FastWan distillation runs (32-64 H200). The run itself, at Wan scale, has no documented price and no open data.

## The derivative ecosystem makes a Wan student cheap, but not better than Wan 2.2

The community has turned Wan 2.1/2.2 into the most-derived open video backbone, and the documented costs of the derivation stage are small. **Self-Forcing** converts Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B into a 4-step causal streaming model, data-free, in "approximately 1.5 hours on 64 H100 GPUs" (the repo says under 2 hours, and "under 16 hours on 8 H100s" with accumulation), reaching VBench 84.31 against the teacher's 84.26 at 17 FPS on one H100 ([arXiv 2506.08009](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08009); [Self-Forcing repo](https://github.com/guandeh17/Self-Forcing)). **LongLive** extends that to minute-long interactive generation in "32 H100 GPU-days" with all training code released ([arXiv 2509.22622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22622); [NVlabs/LongLive](https://github.com/NVlabs/LongLive)). **FastWan** sparse-distils (DMD jointly with VSA) Wan2.1-1.3B in 32 H200 × ~12 h, Wan2.2-TI2V-5B data-free in 64 H200 × ~12 h at full 121×704×1280, and Wan2.1-14B in 64 H200 × ~52 h, all to 3-step inference with recipes, slurm scripts and the 600k synthetic latent set published ([HF FastWan2.1-T2V-1.3B](https://huggingface.co/FastVideo/FastWan2.1-T2V-1.3B-Diffusers); [HF FastWan2.2-TI2V-5B-FullAttn](https://huggingface.co/FastVideo/FastWan2.2-TI2V-5B-FullAttn-Diffusers); [FastVideo DMD docs](https://haoailab.com/FastVideo/distillation/dmd/)). **rCM** distils Wan2.1 up to 14B to 1-4 steps with a published trainer and synthetic dataset, reporting VBench 84.92 for the 4-step 14B student against 83.58 for the teacher, and TurboDiffusion stacks rCM with SLA sparse attention and SageAttention for "100~200×" end-to-end on one RTX 5090 (Wan2.2 I2V A14B 720p: 4549 s → 38 s) ([arXiv 2510.08431](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08431v2); [thu-ml/TurboDiffusion](https://github.com/thu-ml/TurboDiffusion)). **Phased DMD** (the method behind Wan2.2-Lightning v2) trains a rank-64 LoRA generator per expert on 64 GPUs, data-free, and fixes the motion loss of plain DMD2 (dynamic degree 82.27% vs 65.45%) ([arXiv 2510.27684](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.27684v3)). Expressed in GPU-hours, every documented Wan distillation sits between ~100 (Self-Forcing) and ~3,500 (FastWan-14B), and the cost driver at 14B is memory rather than data: DMD holds four 14B copies, and Krea reports that the naive 1.3B Self-Forcing code "runs out of memory at 14B even with FSDP across 64 H100s" ([Krea blog](https://www.krea.ai/blog/krea-realtime-14b)).

Three facts shape what an "own Wan variant" can be. First, the Wan VAE + umT5 pair has become a substrate: SkyReels-V2, HuMo, Phantom and Ovi reuse it, MAGI-2 Preview uses the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B VAE, and LingBot-World 1.x/2.0 are built directly on Wan2.2 I2V-A14B, so latent tooling and kernels written for Wan transfer broadly ([arXiv 2504.13074](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13074); [sand-ai/MAGI-2-preview](https://huggingface.co/sand-ai/MAGI-2-preview); [lingbot-world](https://github.com/robbyant/lingbot-world)). Second, the open recipes are data-free or prompt-only, which removes the data-licensing burden entirely, but the prompt set they all use (VidProM) is CC BY-NC 4.0, so a commercial reproduction needs its own prompts ([HF WenhaoWang/VidProM](https://huggingface.co/datasets/WenhaoWang/VidProM)). Third, the quality ceiling is the teacher: every 1.3B streaming student lands within ~0.6 VBench points of Wan2.1-1.3B, LongLive states its short-clip quality is "unlikely to exceed the base model's", rCM notes few-step students "lag behind the teacher in aspects such as physical consistency", Wan2.2-Lightning warns of artifacts under "extremely large motion", and all causal students accumulate error beyond their training horizon ([arXiv 2509.22622](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.22622); [NVlabs/rcm](https://github.com/NVlabs/rcm); [HF lightx2v/Wan2.2-Lightning](https://huggingface.co/lightx2v/Wan2.2-Lightning)). Since Wan 2.2 (VBench 84.23) is absent from the AA top 29 while Wan 3.0 leads it, a Wan-derived model is a 2025-quality model made fast, long, streaming or cheap; it is not a route toward the 2026 frontier.

The useful observation for fastvideo-rs is what is **not** published. There is a bidirectional 3-step FastWan2.2-5B and a CausalWan2.2 I2V-A14B preview, but no documented causal/streaming recipe for the 5B model, which at ~27k tokens per 720p/24 fps clip is the cheapest Wan to distil and the one MobileWan shows tolerates head pruning but not block pruning ([FastVideo README](https://github.com/hao-ai-lab/FastVideo); [arXiv 2607.06173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06173)). There is no public trainer for 14B causal conversion (Krea released inference only; Self-Forcing's ODE-init instructions are "still pending"; the only documented alternative is Krea's "3k steps on 128 H100 GPUs" causal pre-training) ([Krea blog](https://www.krea.ai/blog/krea-realtime-14b); [Self-Forcing repo](https://github.com/guandeh17/Self-Forcing)). And the lightx2v LoRAs that dominate ComfyUI deployment are weights-only; the Phased DMD paper is their only method description. A variant we train is worth owning where it fills one of those holes or is tuned to our serving path (our shifts, NFE ladder, I2V/FLF2V, commercially clean prompts), not where it re-creates a free checkpoint.

## Only H3 is at the frontier, and the clean-licence tier is 110-200 Elo below it

If the goal is quality rather than speed, the open-weight field in October 2026 is thin at the top. **MiniMax H3** (33B dense single-stream, Qwen3-VL-32B encoder, joint stereo audio, weights 2026-08-03) is the only open model inside the closed frontier band: AA Elo **1158 T2V (#5 overall, 15 points behind Wan 3.0, within the ±16 interval) and 1140 I2V (#1 overall)** ([AA open-weights T2V](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/text-to-video/open-weights); [MiniMax-H3 card](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3)). Its Community License excludes "the European Union, the United Kingdom, the Republic of Korea and the United States of America", requires written authorisation above $20M revenue, mandates a "MiniMax H3" UI notice, and treats distillation as a Model Derivative while forbidding use of outputs "to improve any other artificial intelligence model" ([MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3 LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3/blob/main/LICENSE)). MiniMax ships no training code and no technical report; DiffSynth (full + LoRA), FastVideo (FastH3 distillations, Trim prune) and lightx2v fill the gap, which is exactly the situation our H3 training-cost document already priced ([hao-ai-lab/FastVideo](https://github.com/hao-ai-lab/FastVideo)). The next open tier is **MAGI-2 Preview** (Apache-2.0, 1044 T2V / 1046 I2V) but it is a 114B MoE needing 8 Hopper GPUs and ~307 GB with inference code only, and **LTX-2.5** (22B joint audio-video DiT, 8-step distilled, ~950 T2V / ~984 I2V) whose licence is free below $10M group revenue, ships `ltx-trainer` for full/LoRA/IC-LoRA, but bars using it "to train, improve, or fine-tune any other machine learning model" and publishes no distillation recipe ([sand-ai/MAGI-2-preview](https://huggingface.co/sand-ai/MAGI-2-preview); [LTX-2 LICENSE-2_x](https://github.com/Lightricks/LTX-2/blob/main/LICENSE-2_x); [Lightricks/LTX-2.5](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.5)).

The direction of travel matters as much as today's table. Alibaba, ByteDance (Seedance 2.5 closed) and Kuaishou (Kling 4.0, "no public model weights") keep their flagships API-only, Tencent has shipped nothing newer than HunyuanVideo 1.5 (Nov 2025) in video, and the 2026 open releases come from labs that monetise differently: Lightricks' revenue-tiered licence, Sand.ai's hosted MAGI-2 API, Sber (Kandinsky, MIT), Meituan, Ant/Robbyant, NVIDIA (Cosmos 3 under OpenMDW) and OpenMOSS (MOVA, Apache-2.0 with training code) ([TechTimes 2026-07-31](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/322528/20260731/chinas-ai-video-battle-picks-sides-seedance-25-stays-closed-minimax-h3-goes-open.htm); [morphic Kling 4](https://morphic.com/resources/models/kling-4); [OpenMOSS/MOVA](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOVA)). MiniMax is the lone incumbent that opened a flagship, and even it withholds H3 Max, the 2K regenerate stage and the four largest Western markets. For a Rust engine that already serves Wan 2.x, LTX-2.x and H3, the implication is that future quality upgrades will arrive as new backbones from these second-tier labs, not as a reopened Wan, and the engine's value lies in serving whichever of them wins, plus owning the fast/streaming variants nobody publishes.

## From-scratch training is priced out by data rights before GPUs

The cheapest fully documented from-scratch runs that reach 2025 open-model quality are **SANA-Video-2B** ("12 days on 64 H100 GPUs" = 18,432 H100-hours, VBench 83.71/84.05 vs Wan2.1-14B 83.69/83.73) and **Open-Sora 2.0** (11B, 4,160 H200 GPU-days = 99,840 H200-hours, "$199.6k" at the paper's assumed $2/H200-hour on 70M → 10M → 5M clips) ([arXiv 2509.24695](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24695v2); [arXiv 2503.09642](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.09642)). At October-2026 on-demand prices (H100 SXM $2.60 at GMI to $3.99 at Runpod Secure/Lambda, $5.85 on a Lambda 64-GPU cluster; H200 $3.20 to $6.35) those become **$48k-108k for the 2B run and $320k-634k for the 11B run**, GPU time only, excluding data, captioning, storage, evaluation and failed runs; no provider page offers $2/H200-hour on demand ([Runpod pricing](https://www.runpod.io/pricing); [Lambda pricing](https://lambda.ai/pricing); [GMI Cloud pricing](https://www.gmicloud.ai/pricing); [Nebius prices](https://nebius.com/prices)). Both sub-$250k recipes get there by shrinking tokens (SANA's linear attention and 32× DCAE-V; Open-Sora's Video DC-AE taking a 5 s 768px clip from 76K to 19K tokens) and by training mostly at ≤256p; a dense Wan-style 1.3B at that budget has no published precedent. Seaweed-7B, the one frontier-adjacent model that publishes compute, needed **665,000 H100-hours ($1.7M-3.9M)** on a Θ(100M)-clip pipeline, and Wan 14B at "O(1) trillions of tokens" with no GPU count is unquantifiable but plainly above that ([arXiv 2504.08685](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08685)).

The harder constraint is data. Every large open video-text corpus traces to HD-VILA-100M's R-UDA, which permits the data "solely for Computational Use for non-commercial research" and treats "artificial intelligence models trained on Data" as Results that "may not" enter "any commercial offering"; that covers Panda-70M, Koala-36M and the Panda-derived bulk of OpenVid-1M, while InternVid is CC BY-NC-SA, VidProM CC BY-NC, Vript academic-only and MiraData tagged GPL-3.0 ([HD-VILA-100M LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/XPretrain/main/hd-vila-100m/LICENSE); [Panda-70M](https://github.com/snap-research/Panda-70M); [HF nkp37/OpenVid-1M](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nkp37/OpenVid-1M)). No large open video dataset affirmatively permits commercial model training; every commercial-grade model (Wan, Seaweed, Kandinsky, LTX, SkyReels with its "280,000+ films and 800,000+ TV episodes") describes an in-house or licensed corpus ([arXiv 2504.13074](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13074v3)). A from-scratch model is therefore a licensed-footage and captioning project before it is a GPU project, and none of the public recipes price that part. The multi-node trainers that document from-scratch scale are Open-Sora's ColossalAI stack (11B on 192-224 H200 with CP=4) and NVIDIA NeMo's Megatron diffusion framework (28B DiT at 64k context on 256 H100); FastVideo's FSDP2 + SP claims are credible but its public docs stop at one node, and DiffSynth documents no scale-out ([Open-Sora](https://github.com/hpcaitech/Open-Sora); [NeMo diffusion docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/25.09/vision/diffusionmodels/dit.html); [FastVideo finetune docs](https://haoailab.com/FastVideo/training/finetune/)).

Where compute does go a long way is on top of a Wan checkpoint. Self-Forcing at 96-128 H100-hours is **$250-510**, LongLive at 768 H100-hours is **$2.0k-3.1k**, FastWan2.2-5B's 768 H200-hours is **$2.5k-4.1k**, and even twenty Self-Forcing re-runs for hyper-parameter search stay under $10k; the distilled student routinely scores at or above the teacher on VBench, so the licence exposure is only the teacher's (Apache-2.0 for Wan, contractually blocked for a non-Hunyuan student under Hunyuan's Output clause) and the prompt set's ([arXiv 2506.08009](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08009v2); [arXiv 2509.22622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22622); [HF FastWan2.2-TI2V-5B-FullAttn](https://huggingface.co/FastVideo/FastWan2.2-TI2V-5B-FullAttn-Diffusers); [HunyuanVideo LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent/HunyuanVideo/main/LICENSE.txt)). Budget tiers map cleanly: $10k buys a distilled, streaming or minute-long Wan derivative at teacher quality with a new capability; $100k buys a 2B from-scratch model at Wan2.1-1.3B quality only if SANA's efficiency design is replicated; $1M buys an Open-Sora-2.0-class 11B model at early-2025 quality, below Wan 2.2, with the data problem unsolved.

## Conclusion

The question "can we leverage Wan 3.0" has a short answer and a longer one. The short answer is no: Wan 3.0 is a hosted product whose only public artefacts are an API reference and a price list, and Alibaba's open releases since September 2025 have all been control, animation and dance models on the 2.1/2.2 backbone, which tells us that Tongyi Lab distils with DMD and Self-Forcing internally but nothing about the flagship. The longer answer is that the open Wan line is the best-instrumented substrate in the field for exactly the kind of work fastvideo-rs already does: the derivative ecosystem has reduced a few-step or streaming Wan variant to a data-free, single-pod, three-to-four-figure job with published trainers, and the holes in that ecosystem (a streaming 5B, I2V turbo adapters tuned to a serving ladder, commercially clean prompt sets) are the places where an owned variant is worth more than a downloaded one.

What has changed in 2026 is where quality comes from. The arena shows an open model at the closed frontier for the first time, but it is H3 under a territorial licence, while the incumbents that built the open video field (Alibaba, ByteDance, Kuaishou, Tencent) have stopped shipping weights and the permissive tier is a full generation behind. A team with a fast inference engine should therefore treat model training as a capability and cost lever on top of whichever backbone currently leads, not as a quality race it can enter from scratch: the from-scratch budget that would buy a 2025-class model is dwarfed by the data-rights problem that no public recipe solves, whereas a thousand dollars of distillation on Wan 2.2 or H3 produces something we can serve next month.
