# B200 single-pod benchmark vs RTX PRO 6000

Run date: 2026-09-29. Question: is a B200 cluster worth it against the
RTX PRO 6000 pods we run today? One pod, every serving family in turn, the
same image and configs as production.

**Answer: not for batch serving.** At Runpod's prices a B200 must be
**3.25x** faster than an RTX PRO 6000 to cost the same per clip. It was
**1.2x to 2.1x** faster. Every batch workload costs **1.6x to 2.6x more per clip** on
the B200. The B200 buys latency and memory headroom, not cheaper video.
It is worth renting only for the few cases in the recommendation below.

## Facts recorded first

- **NVENC: none.**
  - ffmpeg lists `h264_nvenc`, but a real encode fails:
    `OpenEncodeSessionEx failed: unsupported device (2)`,
    `No capable devices found`.
  - `nvidia-smi -q` shows encoder stats but no session opens.
  - fv-serve's startup probe logs `NVENC unavailable after the startup
    probe`. It falls back to `engine.post_encoder=cpu-test-x264`,
    `webrtc.encoder=x264-test`, `reactor.h264=off (VP8 only)`, and
    `director.encoder=openh264 (not in this build: answered with VP8
    instead)`.
  - So every MP4 below was encoded with libx264 on the CPU. That cost
    shows up as the `encode` stage: 0.9 s at 480p, 2.5 s at 1080p, 6.1 s
    at 1440p. On an RTX PRO 6000 with NVENC the MP4 tail was 0.02 s.
- **Kernels:** `sm_100 kernels=Cubin(100) gemm=Bf16 resident=true
  sdpa=dense sdpa_chunk=1024`, with the embedded oxide BF16 GEMM tiles
  (`bf16_128x128x128`, `128x256x128`, `256x128x128`, `128x128x256`,
  `f32_128x128x128`). Per family, from the serve log
  (`serve-log-kernels.txt`):

| Family | Weights / GEMM | Attention |
|---|---|---|
| H3 turbo (`h3/fasth3_4step_vsa`) | **MXFP8** on 180 linears (blocks 2..=46, E4M3 + E8M0 per 32 values, cuBLASLt block-scaled: sm_100 has the native kernels, so no W8A8 fallback), BF16 activations; text encoder from the pre-quantized **FP8** tree (`ResidentFp8`) | VSA 0.9: `vsa fine kernel: Tma ring (3 slots) (sm10, 128B swizzle)`; text/refiner `sdpa: fused mma`; Qwen-VL vision `sdpa: device dense` (D=72) |
| H3 max (`h3/sol_h3_4step_engine_ladder`) | MXFP8 + BF16 activations, as turbo | `sol-attn kernel (fused device): thresh_type=Diag tau=1 block=64`; dense step 0 and dense layers 0-1; tau ladder 1 / 1.25 / 1.5 |
| LTX-2.5 turbo (`ltx2/ltx25_distill_sol`) | BF16 (no quantization line) | stage 2: layer 0 dense, layers 1-47 `sol-attn kernel (thresh_type=diag)`; `sdpa: fused mma` for the text path |
| Wan 2.2 5B turbo | **MXFP8** on the attention + FFN linears of all 30 blocks; `nvfp4=off` | `sdpa: fused mma` (Sq=12 555 at 480p) |
| SF-Wan 1.3B | MXFP8 (all 30 blocks) | `sdpa: fused mma`, RebasedSink RoPE, CUDA graphs (71 of 81 blocks replayed) |

**NVFP4** is not used on any served path (Wan logs `nvfp4=off`).
For dense d=128 attention, `wan/attn.rs` `auto` picks `attn_dc` (tcgen05 +
TMEM) on 10.0. The log prints only `sdpa: fused mma` and does not name the
kernel. The sparse kernels that do most of the H3 and LTX attention work
(VSA, Sol-Attn) and the causal path are the generic sm_100 builds, not
tuned datacenter kernels (see Caveats).

## Results

The values are medians of 3 warm jobs, except where n=1. The column
definitions:

- **Run** is the worker's `run_s`, from job start to completion. It is the
  end-to-end GPU occupancy.
- **Wall** is the client's submit to terminal status, through the Runpod
  proxy.
- **Denoise**, **decode** and **encode** are the engine's
  `stage_durations`.
- **Peak** is the engine's `peak_memory_mb`.
- **Util** is the mean `nvidia-smi` GPU utilisation over the job's run
  window.

so the first two H3 turbo cells have no util or smi figure. The prompt was

## Recommendation

1. **Do not build B200 clusters for batch serving** of H3, LTX or Wan.
   - Measured speedups are 1.2-2.1x against a 3.25x price ratio, so every
     batch clip costs 1.6-2.6x more on a B200.
   - H3 turbo 480p/768p, the bulk of traffic, costs 1.8-2.1x more.
   - LTX is the worst fit: 1.1-1.3x at 720p/1080p, with the GPU 30-40%
     busy.
   - The B200 also has no NVENC. That adds 1-6 s of CPU x264 per clip and
     takes H.264 away from the live paths.
   - Scale out on RTX PRO 6000 instead.
2. **Rent a B200 only where the PRO 6000 cannot do the job, or where
   latency is the product:**
   - **SF-Wan live at 16 fps.** The PRO 6000 generates 15.2 fps, below
     playout; the B200 generates 26.9 fps (1.7x headroom). An H100
     (23.8 fps reference, cheaper, but also no NVENC) or an L40S-class card with
     NVENC should be priced before a B200 for this.
   - **H3 10 s 1080p with a resident text encoder.** The PRO 6000 refuses
     this job by design; the B200 runs it at 97-105 GiB with room to
     spare. A streamed-encoder PRO 6000 also admits it, more slowly.
   - **A premium low-latency tier** for H3 max 1080p (46 s instead of
     83 s) or LTX 1440p (62 s instead of 111 s), priced at ~2x per clip.
3. **Revisit** in either of two cases:
   - the B200 price falls below ~$3.5-4/hr, the break-even for H3 and
     Wan at today's speedups;
   - the sm_100 kernel work lands and doubles the speedup. LTX's 30-40%
     utilisation shows the most room.

## Caveats

- **Untuned sm_100 kernels.** The task list has the Sol / VSA / causal
  datacenter kernels as not started.
  - What ran was the generic Cubin(100) builds: the VSA TMA ring, the
    fused sol-attn kernel, and the causal rollout.
  - The GEMM default route is BF16 oxide tiles; H3 and Wan use cuBLASLt
    MXFP8. NVFP4 is off everywhere.
  - The speedups here are a floor for the B200, not a ceiling. The
    biggest gap is LTX (29-38% util), then H3 turbo 1080p (1.40x), where
    the VSA sparse attention dominates.
- **NVENC.** The B200 has no usable NVENC (`unsupported device`), like the
  H100.
  - Batch MP4s fall back to libx264 on the CPU. That adds 0.9-6 s per
    clip to the run, all of it included in the numbers above.
  - With 192 vCPUs this holds up while jobs run one at a time. It would
    not hold up for many concurrent encodes.
  - Live streams lose H.264: WebRTC gets the x264 test encoder, Reactor
    and the director fall back to VP8.
- **Baselines are from other runs.** They used other days, other images
  (sha-9c42844 … 2cd1ba0), other prompts, EU hosts and the EU volume.
  - The H3 PRO numbers are 5-prompt medians from fv-gpucheck `e2e`, or
    worker runs.
  - The LTX PRO numbers are client walls with 2-3 s polls.
  - Nothing here reran the PRO 6000 with image 522af72.
  - The PRO 6000 encodes with NVENC; the B200 did not.
- **n=3 warm medians**, one prompt and one seed per cell (seed 7). The
  1440p, 10 s, i2v and a2v cells are n=1. The run-time spread across the
  three runs was within 5% everywhere.
- **Cold starts depend on the page cache.**
  - h3-max followed h3-turbo on the same `h3-base` tree, so its 84 s is a
    warm-cache figure.
  - The 110 s h3-turbo figure is the one truly cold H3 load on this host.
  - The Wan loads were slow on this volume and host (93-100 s).
- **The GPU util samples before 08:10:57 were lost** (the first two H3
  turbo cells).
- **One host, one data centre** (US-CA-2, stock "Low"). Prices and stock
  change.
